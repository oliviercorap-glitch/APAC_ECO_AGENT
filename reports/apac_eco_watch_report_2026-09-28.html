Executive summary
The APAC ex-China macroeconomic picture is bifurcated, with India and Indonesia in solid manufacturing expansion at PMI 52-58 and strong China+1 FDI inflows, while Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand sit in mild contraction at 48-50 and Vietnam and the Philippines hover near the 50 line. Monetary policy is broadly easing across Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, and India, lowering regional financing costs, but the RBI's October 7 meeting and US$3 billion of pre-emptive Indian debt issuance signal near-term rate uncertainty. Trade developments are mixed: the US-China tariff cut on US$60 billion of goods is mildly positive, but rare earths remain excluded and US Section 301 and reciprocal tariff threats drive a deteriorating six-month trade outlook. The regional risk brief flags a critical unfavorable outlook driven by tariff escalation, potential renewed USD strength, and China growth spillover, which together threaten TLD's APAC revenue, margins, and funding costs. Net impact on TLD APAC operations is cautiously negative on risk, positive on structural opportunity, with priority actions on downside scenario planning, India funding pre-positioning, and rare-earth supply-chain mapping.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Over the next 30-90 days, TLD's regional cost base should remain broadly stable, with easing monetary conditions in Southeast Asia and India offsetting mild currency depreciation and persistent North Asian industrial softness. China+1 FDI momentum into Vietnam, India, Indonesia, and Thailand improves sourcing optionality and medium-term cost competitiveness, though rare-earth input risk and US tariff uncertainty could add unexpected cost volatility.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are improving on balance, with Bank Indonesia, Bank of Thailand, and Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas easing and the BoK and RBA on hold with easing potential, lowering local funding costs for TLD entities and customers over the next 90-180 days. The RBI's October 7 decision and BoJ normalization are the two key exceptions, and renewed USD strength or Fed surprises could reverse the easing tailwind and raise EM Asian funding costs.

Indian firms pre-fund US$3bn debt ahead of RBI October 7 decision

Reading
Indian corporates are lining up roughly US$3 billion of debt issuance in anticipation of a possible RBI rate hike at the October 7 policy meeting, front-loading borrowing before funding costs rise. The pre-emptive issuance signals market expectation of a tightening bias or at minimum a hawkish hold, which would reverse the neutral-to-easing posture noted in the regional brief. This is a sentiment-driven move rather than a confirmed policy shift, but the scale of issuance indicates broad corporate consensus on higher-for-longer Indian rates.
Supply chain impact
Higher Indian funding costs raise the cost of capital for local suppliers and manufacturing partners in TLD's India-linked sourcing chain, potentially slowing capex among component and fabrication vendors. If Indian firms defer expansion, lead times and pricing on locally sourced parts could firm modestly over the next two quarters.
Financing / FX impact
A 25-50bp RBI hike would lift INR financing costs for TLD's India entity and for Indian customers financing GSE leases or purchases, compressing lease affordability. INR has carried a mild depreciation bias versus USD; a hawkish RBI could temporarily support INR but at the cost of higher domestic lending rates, widening the spread TLD pays on local-currency facilities.
Recommended action
Treasury should lock in any pending INR-denominated funding or refinancing before October 7 and stress-test India lease pricing at +50bp; confirm with the India entity whether customer financing pipelines are rate-sensitive this quarter.

APAC PMIs mixed; Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Thailand in mild contraction

Reading
Regional manufacturing PMIs remain divergent: India and Indonesia consistently print in expansion at 52-58, Vietnam and the Philippines hover near the 50 line with periodic dips below, while Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand have spent much of the recent period in mild contraction at 48-50. No single country has breached the CRITICAL threshold of below 48 or above 52 for the first time in 3+ months based on this brief, but the breadth of mild contraction across North Asia is notable. The pattern reflects weak export demand and soft industrial capex in the more mature APAC economies.
Supply chain impact
Mild contraction in Japan, Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand signals softer industrial demand and potentially reduced capex appetite among suppliers and customers in those markets, which could slow order intake for TLD equipment. Expansion in India and Indonesia supports sourcing and customer-base growth there, reinforcing the case for shifting more regional supply-chain weight toward South and Southeast Asia.
Financing / FX impact
Soft PMIs in North Asia typically correlate with easier monetary policy and lower local financing costs, a modest tailwind for TLD entity borrowing in Japan, Korea, and Thailand. However, weak industrial activity can also pressure local currencies and customer credit quality, requiring closer monitoring of lease receivables in contraction markets.
Recommended action
Regional finance should review lease receivable aging and customer credit exposure in Japan, Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand this week, and confirm whether any PMI has crossed below 48 or above 52 for the first time in 3+ months.
