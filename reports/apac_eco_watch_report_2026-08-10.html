Executive summary
The APAC region (ex-Mainland China) presents a mixed but generally manageable economic environment for TLD Group, with one critical exception: Indonesia's rupiah crisis, which has breached critical FX thresholds and requires immediate treasury action. Regional manufacturing conditions remain bifurcated, with strong expansion in India, Vietnam, and Indonesia (PMI 51-58) contrasting with stagnation in Japan and mild contraction in South Korea, while Taiwan's PMI has hit its highest level since 2021 on record exports. The China+1 FDI wave continues to accelerate into ASEAN and India, creating significant opportunities for TLD to expand its manufacturing footprint and supplier base, particularly in Vietnam. Monetary policy is diverging: the RBA's surprise 25bps hike to 4.35% in May 2026 increases Australian financing costs, while the BoJ's gradual tightening to ~0.5% and the BoK's hold at 2.50% reflect a cautious regional stance. RCEP tariff reductions on machinery and parts provide a direct cost benefit for TLD's cross-border component flows. The overall risk outlook is deteriorating due to potential US slowdown, yen volatility, and geopolitical tensions, warranting enhanced stress testing and liquidity buffers.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost competitiveness is improving in Southeast Asia as the China+1 wave deepens supplier ecosystems and infrastructure, while the weak yen (150-155/USD) makes Japanese components cheaper for regional sourcing. The firm AUD and rising Australian rates increase costs in that market, while Indonesia's rupiah crisis creates short-term cost advantages for local sourcing but long-term instability risk.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Financing conditions are broadly stable to improving across most APAC markets, with easing in the Philippines and accommodative stances in Korea and Thailand, but the RBA's hike and BoJ's tightening create pockets of higher costs. Indonesia's emergency rate hike significantly raises borrowing costs in that market, and the rupiah crisis may trigger broader regional risk aversion and tighter credit conditions.
