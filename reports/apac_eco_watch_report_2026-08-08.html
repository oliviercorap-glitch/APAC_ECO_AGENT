Executive summary
The APAC region ex-Mainland China presents a mixed picture in early August 2026. India's manufacturing PMI remains in expansion at 53.5 but the composite PMI fell to a four-year low of 54.3, signaling a sharp slowdown in private-sector activity. South Korea's manufacturing PMI strengthened on semiconductor and automotive demand, while the Philippines saw its PMI rise to a 5-month high of 51.8. However, the Philippines FDI plunged 58.8% YoY to a decade-low of USD 250 million in April, a critical breach of the 15% threshold that demands immediate attention. Indonesia's GDP growth slowed to 5.29%, missing targets, while the rupiah remains under pressure at Rp 17,140 per USD. Vietnam's Q1 FDI reveals shallow "China+1" transshipment plays rather than deep manufacturing investment. US-China trade tensions continue to escalate with new sanctions and tariffs, while the Fed's split on rate hikes keeps US rates elevated, constraining regional monetary policy. Net impact on TLD APAC is a cautious outlook with elevated financing costs and currency volatility, but with pockets of manufacturing strength in South Korea and the Philippines.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional competitiveness over the next 30-90 days will be shaped by continued US dollar strength, which keeps Asian currencies under pressure and supports export competitiveness but raises import and financing costs. India's slowing momentum and Vietnam's shallow FDI suggest the "China+1" shift may be more gradual than headline numbers suggest, while South Korea's strong manufacturing sector indicates robust industrial health in the region.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Financing conditions across APAC will remain tight over the next 90-180 days as the Fed's hawkish stance limits regional central banks' ability to cut rates. The Philippines FDI collapse and Indonesia's growth slowdown could increase country risk premiums, while India's slowing economy may prompt earlier RBI rate cuts. TLD should expect elevated USD financing costs and monitor local currency borrowing opportunities where central banks begin easing.
