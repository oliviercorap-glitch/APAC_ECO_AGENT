Executive summary
The APAC ex-China region faces a sharply deteriorating currency environment, with the Indian Rupee at a record low, the Indonesian Rupiah below 1998 crisis levels, and the Korean Won at a 17-year low, all driven by US-Iran conflict-induced risk-off flows — this is the dominant signal of the period and constitutes a CRITICAL threshold breach across multiple currencies. Manufacturing momentum remains positive but uneven: India PMI at 54.7 (second-slowest in four years), Vietnam at 53.8 with a USD 20.5bn trade surplus, while Indonesia's PMI plummeted in March 2026 on Middle East conflict spillover. India's structural trajectory is strongly positive, with manufacturing GDP share projected to double to 20% by FY2030 and GVA growth accelerating to 9.13% in Q2 FY2025-26. Thailand FDI stands at USD 10.1bn (2024) and Philippine FDI is recovering but trailing prior year, both warranting closer quantification. The net impact on TLD APAC operations is negative on the FX translation and credit risk side, partially offset by continued manufacturing expansion in India and Vietnam supporting medium-term customer capex.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost competitiveness is shifting rapidly as currency depreciation makes India, Indonesia, and South Korea more cost-competitive for export manufacturing but simultaneously raises the local-currency cost of imported EUR and USD-denominated equipment and components. Vietnam and India remain the strongest China+1 beneficiaries, though Vietnam's rare earth dependency on China introduces residual supply chain risk. Over the next 30-90 days, TLD's regional cost base will face upward pressure from imported input costs in depreciating-currency markets.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are tightening materially as currency stress raises sovereign risk premiums and local central banks face pressure to defend currencies through rate hikes, increasing financing costs for TLD's regional entities and customers. IDR stress below 1998 crisis levels is particularly concerning for lease receivable quality in Indonesia, and INR record lows raise similar concerns in India. Over the next 90-180 days, expect elevated hedging costs, wider credit spreads on local-currency lease originations, and potential capital flow management measures in the most stressed markets.
