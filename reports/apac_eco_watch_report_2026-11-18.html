Executive summary
APAC ex-China manufacturing is broadly expansionary, with India at 54.7, Japan at 53.3, Singapore at 51.4, and Malaysia growing 5.5% on a tech boom, though momentum is softening at the margin. Currency stress is the dominant theme: the Indian rupee sits near record lows around 90.9 per dollar on $2.5 billion of portfolio outflows, Indonesia hiked rates to defend the rupiah, and the Philippines saw $4.3 billion in hot-money outflows with its FDI forecast cut to $1.8 billion from $3.7 billion. South Korea held at 2.5% while lifting growth, and the BSP is easing cautiously. Net impact on TLD APAC is mixed: solid industrial demand supports customer capex, but EM currency weakness raises FX risk on EUR/USD-denominated GSE financing and pressures translation of regional results. Priority actions are INR and PHP hedging review within 48 hours and repricing of Indonesian customer lease assumptions.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional manufacturing competitiveness remains favorable, with India's weaker rupee and Malaysia's lagging wages enhancing cost advantages for China+1 sourcing over the next 30-90 days. Japan's softening PMI and Singapore's moderating expansion suggest cyclical cooling in the more mature markets, while India and Malaysia lead growth.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
EM Asia financing conditions are bifurcating: India and the Philippines face elevated country risk premiums and FX volatility, raising customer financing costs, while South Korea's stable 2.5% rate and Malaysia's solid growth keep credit conditions supportive. Expect continued currency-driven hedging cost increases over 90-180 days, with Indonesia's tightening adding local cost pressure.
