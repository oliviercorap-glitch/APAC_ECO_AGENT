Important 🌐 TRADE Regional

China September exports surge 36.3% YoY, imports up 31%, near US$1tn surplus

Reading
China's September exports jumped 36.3% year on year while imports rose 31%, putting the annual trade surplus on track to exceed US$1 trillion for the first time. The surge reflects front-loading ahead of tariff deadlines, weak domestic demand compressing import volumes relative to exports, and continued competitiveness in electronics, machinery and EV supply chains. The lopsided external position intensifies pressure on trading partners and raises the probability of further tariff and anti-dumping actions across APAC.
Supply chain impact
A US$1 trillion Chinese surplus signals continued aggressive export pricing of components and finished industrial goods into Southeast Asia, South Asia and Oceania, which pressures regional suppliers and could accelerate China+1 relocation of TLD's component sourcing into Vietnam, Thailand and India. TLD should expect continued availability of low-cost Chinese components but rising tariff risk on any China-origin content embedded in regional assembly.
Financing / FX impact
Persistent Chinese surpluses typically support regional current account surpluses in ASEAN and pressure local currencies to appreciate versus a weak CNY, marginally raising TLD's regional cost base when translated to EUR. Expect heightened FX volatility in THB, PHP, IDR and MYR as trading partners adjust to Chinese export competition.
Recommended action
CFO APAC to request a 30-day review of TLD's China-origin component exposure across APAC entities and quantify tariff sensitivity; treasury to model a 2-3% appreciation scenario in THB, PHP and IDR versus EUR.

Watch 🏦 MONETARY Regional

PBOC buys gold 23 straight months, largest addition in three years

Reading
China's central bank has added gold for 23 consecutive months, with the latest purchase the largest in three years, as part of a deliberate pivot away from US dollar reserves. The move reflects Beijing's desire to reduce exposure to dollar-denominated assets and sanctions risk, and it is being mirrored by several other Asian central banks. Sustained official-sector gold buying supports elevated gold prices and signals a structural shift in reserve composition across the region.
Supply chain impact
No direct supply chain impact, though a weaker dollar environment implied by de-dollarisation could modestly reduce input cost inflation for USD-denominated commodities used in TLD's manufacturing.
Financing / FX impact
De-dollarisation by Asian central banks is a medium-term signal for greater FX reserve diversification, which can increase volatility in USD/Asia pairs and complicate TLD's USD-denominated intercompany funding and lease receivables. Gold-heavy reserve strategies may also reduce central bank appetite for holding large USD buffers, subtly raising sovereign funding costs in the region.
Recommended action
Treasury to monitor Asian central bank reserve composition disclosures monthly and stress-test TLD's USD receivables book against a 3-5% USD depreciation scenario over the next two quarters.

IMF cuts China 2026 GDP forecast, warns export reliance unsustainable

Reading
The IMF's latest World Economic Outlook revised China's 2026 GDP projection downward and cautioned that export strength alone cannot sustain growth given weak domestic demand and property sector drag. The downgrade implies softer Chinese import demand for regional intermediate goods and commodities, and reduced spillover growth to ASEAN, South Korea, Japan and Australia. Supply chain impact

Conversely, continued Chinese export competitiveness may accelerate relocation of manufacturing capacity into Vietnam, Thailand and India, expanding TLD's addressable customer base in those markets.
Financing / FX impact
A weaker Chinese growth outlook supports further PBOC easing, keeping CNY soft and indirectly pressuring other Asian currencies to remain competitive, which is mildly negative for TLD's EUR-reported regional results. Lower regional growth may also prompt rate cuts in South Korea, Thailand and Indonesia, reducing TLD's local borrowing costs but signalling weaker customer credit quality.
Recommended action
CFO APAC to incorporate a 30-50bps downside scenario to regional GDP into the Q4 2026 forecast and review customer credit exposure in commodity-linked sectors across Australia, Indonesia and Malaysia within two weeks.
