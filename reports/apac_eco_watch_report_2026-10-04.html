Executive summary
The APAC ex-China economic picture is mixed but tilted toward expansion, with Thailand's manufacturing PMI breaching 54.3 (highest since December 2025) signaling robust industrial momentum, even as the World Bank cuts Thailand's 2026 GDP growth to 1.7% on weak exports. Currency volatility is the dominant risk theme: the Thai baht has surged to a five-year high, Japan conducted its first coordinated yen-buying intervention with the US since 2011, and Ackman's bet against the Hong Kong dollar peg signals growing market concern about regional currency stability. Trade architecture is shifting rapidly, with US tariffs on Chinese goods exceeding 100% and CPTPP/RCEP frameworks creating new opportunities for TLD to optimize cross-border component and equipment flows. Net impact on TLD APAC operations is moderately negative in the near term due to FX translation headwinds in Thailand and Japan, but positive in the medium term as supply chain diversification creates new sourcing and customer opportunities across Vietnam, Indonesia, and India. Priority actions are immediate FX hedging reviews for Thailand and Japan entities, a multi-country supply chain footprint assessment, and CPTPP/RCEP tariff optimization.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
TLD's APAC cost base faces near-term pressure from baht and yen appreciation, which raises the EUR-denominated cost of Thai and Japanese operations and components. However, Thailand's strong PMI and the broader China+N diversification trend create offsetting opportunities for TLD to expand assembly and service capacity in lower-cost APAC markets. Net regional competitiveness is expected to remain stable over the next 30-90 days, with margin pressure in Thailand and Japan offset by efficiency gains from CPTPP/RCEP trade facilitation.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are expected to remain accommodative over the next 90-180 days, with New Zealand holding rates low and most APAC central banks unlikely to tighten aggressively given subdued growth. However, FX volatility in Thailand and Japan will increase hedging costs and may widen credit spreads for customers with export exposure. TLD should expect stable but slightly higher funding costs in Japan and Thailand, with opportunities for cheaper financing in Vietnam, Indonesia, and India as those markets deepen.
