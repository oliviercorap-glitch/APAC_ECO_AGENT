Executive summary
APAC ex-China manufacturing is broadly expansionary, with Japan at 54.9, India at 56.20, Indonesia at 54.0, and Vietnam at 52.3 all above the 52 threshold, while the Philippines at 49.1 is the clear laggard in contraction territory. Monetary policy is mixed: South Korea holds at 3.00%, Australia holds with inflation above 3% through 2026, and New Zealand signals future hikes at 2.25%. Currency pressure is a theme, with the Indian rupee weakening on outflows and a dovish RBI, and Indonesia's high FX debt (~30% of GDP) a structural vulnerability. The China+1 diversification trend is accelerating among EU firms, expanding TLD's sourcing and customer opportunities across Southeast and South Asia. Priority actions are the Philippines contraction review, Indonesia and Japan threshold-breach confirmations, and INR/IDR hedging.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional manufacturing competitiveness is strengthening across Japan, India, Indonesia, and Vietnam, supporting supplier availability and customer capex appetite for the next 30-90 days. The Philippines stands out as a weakening market requiring tighter credit management. Currency divergence, with INR and IDR soft and TWD resilient, will shift relative cost bases within the region.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Financing conditions are bifurcated: Korea and Australia hold at elevated rates keeping local borrowing costs high, while New Zealand signals a possible hiking turn. India's dovish RBI lowers domestic rates but rupee weakness offsets the benefit. Expect higher-for-longer costs in Oceania and currency-driven risk premium pressure in India and Indonesia over 90-180 days.
