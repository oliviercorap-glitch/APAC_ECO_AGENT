Executive summary
APAC ex-China manufacturing is broadly expansionary but diverging, with Thailand (54.8), Taiwan (54.9), Japan (53.3), Vietnam (52.3), and Malaysia (51.0) above the 52 threshold, while South Korea decelerated sharply to 51.3 and the Philippines breached into contraction below 48 for a second month. Vietnam FDI surged to USD 24.08 billion, a near-15% swing warranting escalation, and Indonesia drew USD 6.97 billion in portfolio inflows despite rupiah weakness. The RBA hiked 25bps to 4.85%, an 18-year high and fifth hike of 2026, raising financing costs across Oceania, while the RBNZ held at a below-forecast 2.75%. Currency pressure is building across the region — INR toward 91.65, IDR weak, THB appreciating sharply enough to trigger central bank intervention, and HKD in recession. Net impact on TLD APAC is mixed: strong Southeast Asian manufacturing supports customer capex, but rising Australian rates, FX volatility, and Philippines contraction require active treasury and credit management.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost competitiveness is shifting toward weaker-currency markets — India, Indonesia, and the Philippines — as sourcing bases, while appreciating THB and strong JPY raise cost bases in Thailand and Japan. Vietnam's FDI surge reinforces its China+1 competitiveness, and TLD should tilt sourcing toward lower-cost, weaker-currency markets over the next 30-90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Australian financing costs are rising with the RBA at 4.85% and more hikes flagged, while New Zealand and much of Southeast Asia remain accommodative. Expect tighter AUD credit conditions and continued FX volatility across INR, IDR, and THB over the next 90-180 days, warranting active hedging and customer FX-indexation review.
