Executive summary
APAC ex-China manufacturing is in broad mild expansion, with India leading at 10.7% FY26 GVA growth and PMIs in the mid-to-high 50s, Vietnam at 52.8, Japan at 53.3, Taiwan at 52.8, Singapore at 51.4 and Malaysia returning to 51.0, while Indonesia expands for a second month and Thailand attracts THB 153.6bn FDI up 73% year-on-year. Monetary policy is mostly easing or on hold, with the BoK paused at 3.00% after 50bp of hikes and the RBA hawkish at 3.60%, while the BoJ remains the outlier normalizing policy amid yen weakness beyond 163 per dollar. China+1 FDI continues to flow into Vietnam, India, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia, supporting TLD's regional customer base and sourcing options. The principal downside risks are US tariff escalation, renewed USD strength, a sharper China slowdown and geopolitical flashpoints. Priority actions are Japan FX hedging review, Thailand growth resourcing, and tariff and compliance exposure assessment.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
The regional cost base is broadly stable, with easing bias in India, Thailand, Indonesia and the Philippines lowering local financing costs while Australian and Korean rates stay elevated. China+1 FDI and RCEP/CPTPP tariff reduction improve cross-border sourcing economics, though US sector-specific duties on steel and aluminum add input cost risk. Net regional competitiveness is modestly favorable over the next 30-90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are improving as most APAC central banks hold or ease, with the RBI, BoT, BI and BSP having room to cut over 90-180 days. The BoK pause at 3.00% and RBA hawkish hold at 3.60% keep Korean and Australian costs elevated, while BoJ normalization raises Japan costs. Overall credit conditions are stable to modestly easier outside Japan, Korea and Australia.
