Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is broadly neutral on industrial activity but deteriorating on risk: regional manufacturing PMIs are mixed with India and Vietnam in solid expansion, Taiwan and South Korea recovered into mild expansion on the AI cycle, and Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, and the Philippines hovering around or just below 50, with the Philippines at 50.9 in June. FDI is a clear bright spot, with Ho Chi Minh City alone drawing USD 17.8bn in 9M 2026 and surpassing its annual target, while China+1 diversification continues to channel capital into Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and India. The offsetting negatives are FX depreciation pressure across most APAC currencies against a firm USD, rising US tariff uncertainty that undercuts RCEP/CPTPP gains, and a deteriorating six-month risk outlook. For TLD, the net effect is stable industrial demand with improving local financing conditions from the APAC easing cycle, offset by translation and transaction FX risk on euro-denominated equipment and rising compliance burdens in Thailand and Indonesia. Priority actions are FX exposure quantification against the 3% threshold, HKD peg stress-testing, and transfer-pricing and local-content compliance reviews.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost bases are under mild pressure from local currency depreciation against a firm USD, which raises the local cost of imported components and euro-denominated equipment, while a stronger TWD marginally erodes Taiwanese supplier competitiveness. The China+1 build-out in Vietnam, India, and Indonesia continues to expand the addressable customer base and sourcing options, keeping the net regional competitiveness outlook stable to modestly favorable over the next 30-90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
The APAC easing cycle led by Bank Indonesia, Bank of Thailand, and the Philippines' BSP is gradually lowering local-currency financing costs for TLD entities and customers over the next 90-180 days, a demand tailwind for GSE leasing. Offsetting this, BOJ normalization raises yen funding costs, FX volatility against a firm USD raises hedging costs, and trade-policy uncertainty can widen country risk premia in the most US-exposed markets.

Reading
