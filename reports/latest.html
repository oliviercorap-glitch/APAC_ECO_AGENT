Executive summary
The APAC ex-China region presents a bifurcated picture, with strong FDI and manufacturing expansion in Vietnam (PMI 52.3, FDI US$54.85bn through October) and India (net FDI up fourfold to $6.58bn in April) contrasting sharply with the Philippines, where net FDI collapsed to an 11-year low of $210M in May. Thailand's manufacturing PMI rose to 54.3, its highest since December 2025, while the Bank of Thailand signals room for rate cuts and moves to ease baht strength via a repatriation threshold increase to $10M. Most regional central banks are in easing or hold mode, lowering local financing costs, though the RBA holds at 4.60% and the BOJ normalizes gradually. Broad APAC currency depreciation versus a resilient USD is a translation headwind for TLD's regional entity results in EUR, and the six-month risk outlook is unfavorable on US tariff volatility, USD strength, and China spillover. Priority actions are the Philippine exposure review within 48 hours, Vietnam and India opportunity assessments, and regional FX hedging review.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional competitiveness is improving in Vietnam, India, and Thailand on strong FDI and manufacturing expansion, supporting TLD's sourcing and customer-base opportunities, while the Philippines deteriorates on collapsing FDI. Currency depreciation across most APAC markets raises imported component costs but improves export competitiveness for TLD's regional manufacturing footprint over the next 30-90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are broadly improving as most APAC central banks ease or hold, lowering local-currency borrowing costs for TLD entities and customers in Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, and South Korea over the next 90-180 days. However, USD strength, FX volatility, and elevated Australian rates sustain hedging costs and translation risk, and the Philippine FDI collapse raises country risk premium concerns.
