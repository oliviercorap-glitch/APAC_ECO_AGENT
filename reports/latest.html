Reading
The Indian rupee's recent recovery is under pressure from elevated oil prices and persistent capital outflows, with the rebound partly engineered by Reserve Bank of India measures to curb US dollar demand. The RBI's administrative intervention suggests underlying depreciation pressure remains significant, and the currency's trajectory will depend on whether oil prices stabilise and portfolio flows return.
Supply chain impact
A weaker rupee reduces India's import costs in local terms for TLD's India-sourced components and any locally manufactured GSE sub-assemblies, but raises the cost of imported inputs and EUR-denominated equipment. If depreciation resumes, TLD's India operations could see input cost inflation on imported parts, partially offsetting labour cost advantages.
Financing / FX impact
Rupee depreciation versus EUR increases the translation cost of TLD's India entity results when reported in EUR and raises the local-currency cost of any EUR- or USD-denominated intercompany funding. Customer financing conditions for GSE leases in India could tighten if the RBI is forced to defend the currency with higher rates or tighter liquidity.
