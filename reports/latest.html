Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is bifurcated between a firm North Asian industrial base and stressed conditions in India and Australia. Japan manufacturing PMI at 53.3 remains above the 52 expansion threshold though decelerating from 54.1, Malaysia reports renewed output and order growth, and Hong Kong posted 5.9% year-on-year Q1 GDP growth, its strongest in nearly five years. Against this, the Indian rupee has breached 90 per USD with net FDI negative for a second consecutive month, Thailand is tightening gold trading controls to counter a baht that rose 9% in 2025 and a further 1.5% in 2026, and Australia's RBA has delivered a fifth 2026 hike to 4.85%, the highest policy rate in the G20. Net impact on TLD APAC is negative on financing costs and translation, positive on North Asian supplier reliability. Priority actions are repricing Australian leases, stress-testing Indian receivables, and reviewing THB and TWD translation exposure.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Thailand's cost base is eroding on a 9%-plus baht appreciation while India's sub-90 rupee improves Indian manufacturing competitiveness but is undermined by negative net FDI and tariff pressure. Japan and Malaysia remain the most stable manufacturing cost environments in the region over the next 30-90 days, with Taiwan's semiconductor-led FDI concentration offering limited spillover to general industrial supply chains.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Australian financing conditions are the tightest in the region at 4.85% and will raise lease pricing and customer credit risk over the next 90-180 days, while Hong Kong's 5.9% growth and expanding trade finance capacity provide a partial offset for regional working capital. Indian external position deterioration and negative net FDI raise the probability of further rupee weakness and tighter local credit for INR-denominated equipment financing.

Watch ⚖️ COMPLIANCE Philippines

Philippines BSP foreign investment registration rules shape capital repatriation

Reading
Guidance on Philippine foreign investment registration confirms that peso-funded investments from existing local accounts generally do not require BSP registration because no foreign exchange enters the banking system, while other transfers may fall outside registration requirements. The distinction matters because BSP registration is what secures the ability to repatriate capital and remit dividends at the official rate.
Supply chain impact
No direct supply chain impact.
Financing / FX impact
Unregistered inward investment can restrict TLD's ability to repatriate capital or remit profits from Philippine operations through the banking system, creating a treasury and dividend-planning constraint. Any capital injection into the Philippine entity should be routed through BSP-registered channels to preserve FX access.
Recommended action
Treasury to confirm within the week that all capital injections and intercompany funding into the Philippine entity are BSP-registered, and document the registration trail for future repatriation.
