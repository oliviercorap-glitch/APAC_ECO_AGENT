Executive summary
The APAC ex-China region presents a mixed but broadly resilient picture as of late November 2026, with manufacturing PMIs across India (54.7), Indonesia (51.2), and Thailand holding in expansion territory while South Korea's BOK holds at 3% after 50bps of cumulative hikes and upgrades growth forecasts. The dominant near-term risk is trade policy: the US has proposed tariffs of up to 20% on Singapore among 14 partners, though analysts expect the US-China truce to hold if duties remain capped. FX pressure is building in Thailand (baht up 2.5% in November, BOT intervening) and Taiwan (over US$10bn foreign outflows in November), while Australia's RBA retains a hawkish tilt at 3.60%. Thailand's THB 1.7 trillion FDI pipeline and Foreign Business Act liberalization present a clear structural opportunity for TLD's regional footprint. Priority actions are tariff exposure mapping for Singapore, FX hedge reviews for Thailand and Taiwan, and financing cost stress-testing for South Korea and Australia.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost bases are under mild upward pressure from baht and won strength and tight Australian and Korean labor markets, while Thailand's FDI surge and liberalization improve its relative attractiveness as a manufacturing and treasury hub. India and Indonesia remain solid expansion markets supporting customer capex appetite. Net regional competitiveness is stable to slightly positive for TLD over the next 30-90 days, with FX translation the main headwind.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are tightening at the margin, with South Korea's 3% policy rate and hawkish bias, Australia's 3.60% hold with hike discussion, and Korean deposit rates at 3.57% raising customer and entity funding costs. Thailand's 2% rate and Indonesia's stable stance provide offsetting low-cost pockets. Over 90-180 days, expect higher-for-longer rates in Korea and Australia and continued FX-driven volatility in Thailand and Taiwan.
