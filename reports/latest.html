Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is broadly expansionary but uneven: Thailand's manufacturing PMI breached the 52 threshold at 54.3 (highest in series), Vietnam eased to 51.9 but stayed expansionary, and Malaysia held a mild 50.2. Currency stress is the dominant risk — Taiwan's dollar fell sharply on foreign institutional outflows (a likely >3% move requiring 48-hour verification), Indonesia's rupiah traded a wide Rp15,300–16,750 band, and India's net FDI remains structurally weak at USD 6.95bn despite recovery. Regulatory tightening in Japan's FDI screening regime adds deal-timing risk, while RCEP-CPTPP dialogue offers a medium-term trade-friction tailwind. Net impact on TLD APAC is modestly positive on demand (Thai and Vietnamese industrial strength) but negative on FX translation and hedging costs. Priority actions are TWD exposure verification, IDR hedging review, and Japan screening assessment.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional manufacturing competitiveness is improving, led by Thailand's strong expansion and Vietnam's continued growth, supporting supplier reliability and customer capex appetite. Currency weakness in Taiwan and Indonesia lowers local cost bases in hard-currency terms but raises imported-equipment costs, creating a mixed but net-favorable cost environment for the next 30–90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Financing conditions are stable to slightly tighter: FX volatility in Taiwan, Indonesia, and India raises hedging and country-risk premia, while strong Thai activity supports customer credit quality there. Expect elevated hedging costs and more conservative INR/IDR lending terms over the next 90–180 days absent currency stabilization.

Watch 🔧 REFORM Japan

Japan tightens FDI screening regime under amended FX and Foreign Trade Act

Reading
Amendments promulgated 5 June 2026 further enhance Japan's foreign direct investment screening regime, expanding review scope and raising penalties (up to three times the value of the subject matter where it exceeds one million yen). This continues a multi-year trend of tighter inbound investment scrutiny in sensitive sectors.
Supply chain impact
Any TLD expansion of Japanese manufacturing, JV, or sourcing footprint could face longer pre-clearance timelines and sector-specific review; existing operations are unaffected but new incorporations or acquisitions require early screening assessment.
Financing / FX impact
No direct FX impact, but deal-timing risk rises for any Japan-based M&A or capital injection, potentially delaying intercompany funding deployment and increasing advisory costs.
Recommended action
Legal and treasury to map whether TLD's Japanese activities fall within screened sectors and build screening lead-time into any 2027 Japan investment planning this week.

Important 🌍 FDI India

India net FDI recovered to USD 6.95bn FY2025-26 but remains far below peak

Reading
Net FDI fell from USD 27.99bn in FY2022-23 to USD 10.13bn (FY2023-24) and USD 0.96bn (FY2024-25), recovering only to USD 6.95bn in FY2025-26, while gross inflows continued to grow. The gap signals heavy repatriation and outflows offsetting new investment, and Q1 FY2026-27 equity inflow data shows continued modest momentum.
Supply chain impact
Weak net FDI signals cautious foreign capital commitment to Indian manufacturing despite China+1 rhetoric, tempering the pace of new supplier and customer base formation for TLD in India.
Financing / FX impact
Persistent net FDI weakness is a structural INR vulnerability, raising hedging costs and country risk premium relevant to any INR-denominated customer financing or intercompany lending.
Recommended action
Country finance lead to monitor INR and RBI response; factor a conservative INR assumption into any India customer financing proposals this quarter.
