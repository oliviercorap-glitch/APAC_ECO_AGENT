India September CPI jumps to 5.54%, food inflation 7.74% fuels rate-hike case

Reading

This strengthens the case for the RBI to tighten further or at minimum abandon any easing bias, reversing the neutral-to-easing stance noted in the regional policy brief.
Supply chain impact
Higher Indian rates and sticky inflation raise the cost of working capital and local supplier financing for any TLD sourcing or assembly activity in India, and could slow the capex appetite of Indian customers and distributors. No immediate disruption to component flows, but input-cost pressure across Indian light manufacturing is likely to persist.
Financing / FX impact
A hawkish RBI supports the INR, modestly reducing translation losses on TLD's Indian entity results reported in EUR, but raises local borrowing costs for TLD India and for customers financing GSE purchases or leases. Expect higher INR funding spreads and possible repricing of existing floating-rate facilities.
Recommended action
CFO APAC should stress-test TLD India's floating-rate exposure and customer lease pricing against a further 25-50bps RBI hike within the week, and review INR hedge ratios given the hawkish tilt.

Important 📊 PMI Regional

APAC PMIs mixed — India/Indonesia expand, Japan/Korea/Taiwan/Thailand sub-50

Reading
Regional manufacturing PMIs remain bifurcated: India and Indonesia are in solid expansion, Vietnam and the Philippines hover near the 50 line with modest gains, while Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand have spent much of the recent period in sub-50 contraction or stagnation on weak electronics demand. This is a persistent divergence rather than a fresh threshold breach, but the Northeast Asian sub-50 cluster is a drag on regional industrial sentiment.
Supply chain impact
TLD's Northeast Asian supplier and component base (Japan, Korea, Taiwan) faces soft demand and possible capacity underutilization, which could ease component pricing and lead times but also signals weaker regional industrial capex appetite. Southeast Asian expansion markets (India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines) offer relatively stronger sourcing and customer-base momentum.
Financing / FX impact
Divergent PMIs imply divergent central bank paths — easing bias in ASEAN, caution in Northeast Asia — affecting relative local funding costs and FX carry for TLD entities. Weak Northeast Asian manufacturing typically correlates with softer local currencies, a modest translation headwind for JPY/KRW/TWD-reported results.
Recommended action
CFO APAC should tilt working-capital and inventory planning toward expanding Southeast/South Asian markets and review Northeast Asian entity cost bases for efficiency within the week.

APAC currencies hold mild depreciation bias vs USD; JPY volatile on BoJ

Reading

Financing / FX impact

BoJ normalization remains the key JPY risk, with potential for sharp appreciation and hedging-cost spikes.

RCEP cumulation rules make it easier for TLD to source components across ASEAN, Japan, Korea, and Australia and still qualify for preferential tariffs, reducing landed costs and customs complexity for cross-border equipment and component flows. CPTPP expansion opens additional duty-free channels for TLD's Oceania and ASEAN operations.

Lower tariff and customs costs improve gross margins on cross-border equipment flows and reduce the cash tied up in duty and clearance processes, a modest working-capital benefit. No direct FX impact, though smoother trade flows support regional currency stability.
Recommended action
