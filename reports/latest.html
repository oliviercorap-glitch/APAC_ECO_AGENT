Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is mixed, with manufacturing momentum diverging sharply across the region: South Korea's PMI fell 2.6 points to 51.3 (an 8-month low), Indonesia rose to 54.0, and the Philippines slipped below 50 for only the third time on record. The dominant macro event is the RBA's fifth 2026 hike, taking the cash rate to 4.85%, the highest since 2008, driven by an oil-shock inflation surge. Vietnam continues to attract China+1 FDI, though much of it appears to be shallow transshipment-adjacent relocation rather than deep greenfield investment. Net impact on TLD APAC is a higher cost of capital in Australia and softer customer capex appetite in Korea and the Philippines, offset by resilient Indonesian demand. Priority actions are repricing Australian lease terms and confirming the Philippine PMI reading against the 48 threshold.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Regional cost bases are under mild upward pressure from Australian rate hikes and oil-driven inflation, while Korean and Philippine industrial softening reduces near-term customer capex appetite. Indonesia and Vietnam remain the relative bright spots for manufacturing competitiveness and sourcing diversification over the next 30-90 days.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Australian financing conditions are tightening materially with the cash rate at an 18-year high, and Korean and Philippine softening may ease local rates modestly. Regional credit conditions are diverging, so TLD should expect higher AUD funding costs and stable-to-easing conditions in Korea and Indonesia over the next 90-180 days.
