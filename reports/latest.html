Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is stable but uneven, with India manufacturing PMI at 54.2 and Vietnam in solid expansion offsetting near-50 readings in Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, and the Philippines (Philippines 50.1). The standout risk is Indonesia, where the rupiah has breached Rp 17,400/USD—weakest since 1997-98—with FX reserves down over US$8 billion and capital outflows of nearly US$1.6 billion, a clear CRITICAL currency breach. A stronger-for-longer USD is pressuring JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, IDR, and INR, raising translation and USD-linked funding costs for TLD entities, while most central banks are easing or on hold (BoJ the normalization outlier), a mild financing tailwind. China+1 FDI continues to favor Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and India, creating sourcing and customer opportunities, but US tariff escalation and political/fiscal risk in Thailand, the Philippines, and Indonesia make the six-month outlook unfavorable. Priority actions are the Indonesia FX stress test and hedging review within 48 hours, a regional FX hedging refresh, and a six-month risk stress test.

🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)

Net conditions are mixed with downside risk concentrated in Indonesia and USD-linked exposure.

Critical 💱 FX Indonesia

ALERT: IDR breaches Rp 17,400/USD, weakest since 1997-98 crisis

Reading
The Indonesian rupiah has weakened past Rp 17,400 per US dollar, a level not seen since the Asian Financial Crisis of 1997-98. FX reserves fell by over US$8 billion between January and April 2026, and capital outflows hit nearly US$1.6 billion in the first weeks of January alone. This is a clear currency-move threshold breach well beyond 3% and a reserve-drawdown signal of elevated country risk.
Supply chain impact
A sharply weaker rupiah raises the local-currency cost of imported components and equipment for any TLD Indonesia-linked sourcing or inventory, and increases pricing volatility for locally denominated supplier contracts.
Financing / FX impact
TLD's Indonesia entity faces material translation losses on EUR-reported results, higher USD-linked funding costs, and elevated counterparty/country risk premium; customer financing in IDR becomes more expensive and lease affordability deteriorates.
Recommended action
CFO APAC to review Indonesia entity FX exposure and hedging within 48 hours, assess intercompany funding currency mix, and stress-test IDR depreciation scenarios against local lease book.
