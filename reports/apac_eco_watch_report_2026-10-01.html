Executive summary
APAC ex-China macroeconomic conditions are broadly expansionary but increasingly divergent, with India leading at 7.8% GDP growth and a 52.8 PMI, Thailand accelerating to 53.6, Japan's manufacturing PMI at a 45-month high, and Singapore steady at 51.3, while Indonesia (51.43) and the Philippines (50.7) show only marginal expansion and Vietnam faces cost-driven slowdown. Monetary policy is turning more restrictive in several markets, with the Bank of Korea hiking 25bps to 2.75%, the RBA raising 25bps to a 15-year high of 4.60%, and the Bank of Japan normalisation risk rising, while Bank Indonesia holds at 5.75% and New Zealand's easing cycle nears its end. FX conditions are dominated by US-Japan intervention and persistent yen weakness, introducing two-sided JPY risk, while Indonesia's US$8.5bn Q2 portfolio inflows and US$3.55bn August trade surplus support the rupiah. Net impact on TLD APAC operations is moderately positive for demand but negative for financing costs, with higher local rates in Korea and Australia raising customer GSE financing costs and TLD entity borrowing expenses. Priority actions are to reprice Korean and Australian credit facilities and customer lease rates, review JPY hedge ratios given intervention-driven volatility, and reassess regional sourcing toward higher-momentum markets like Thailand and India.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Over the next 30-90 days, TLD's regional cost base faces upward pressure from rising Korean and Australian financing costs and Vietnamese input cost inflation, while Japanese sourcing costs remain favourable on yen weakness. Thailand and India offer the strongest relative competitiveness for sourcing and customer demand, whereas Indonesia and the Philippines offer limited near-term momentum. Net regional competitiveness is stable to slightly positive, contingent on managing FX and rate-driven cost increases.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are tightening modestly over the next 90-180 days, with Korea at 2.75% and Australia at 4.60% and further hikes possible, raising both TLD entity borrowing costs and customer GSE lease financing costs in those markets. Indonesia (5.75%) and Thailand remain relatively accommodative, supporting customer financing demand, while Japan's normalisation path and yen intervention introduce uncertainty for JPY funding. Treasury should prioritise repricing Korean and Australian facilities and reviewing JPY hedge ratios.
