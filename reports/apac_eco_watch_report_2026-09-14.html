Executive summary
The APAC ex-China macroeconomic picture is bifurcated but net constructive: India and Vietnam manufacturing PMIs hold in the 52-58 expansion band, China+1 FDI continues to accelerate into Vietnam, India, Indonesia, Thailand, and Malaysia, and most regional central banks are pausing or easing, with BOK, BI, BOT, and BSP cutting or signaling cuts. Offsetting this, an oil-driven revival of Fed rate-hike bets is strengthening the USD and pressuring the yen, won, baht, and rupiah, while Taiwan faces pressure to hike with GDP forecast above 11%. Trade architecture is improving through RCEP deepening, CPTPP UK accession, and India-EFTA and India-UAE deals, but data localization, transfer pricing scrutiny, and ESG disclosure rules are raising compliance costs. No CRITICAL threshold breaches were triggered today: no PMI below 48 or above 52 for the first time in 3+ months, no FX move above 3% in 30 days, no policy rate move above 50bps, and no FDI swing above 15% YoY. Net impact on TLD APAC operations is modestly positive on sourcing and customer financing, with the principal near-term risk being a hawkish Fed surprise that would reverse the regional easing window and widen FX translation losses.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
TLD's APAC cost base should improve modestly over the next 30-90 days as China+1 supplier depth in Vietnam, India, and Thailand expands and RCEP rules-of-origin flexibility lowers cross-border customs costs. The main offset is a structurally weak yen and Fed-sensitive won, baht, and rupiah, which introduce translation volatility into EUR-reported results. Overall regional competitiveness is stable to slightly favorable.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are the most favorable in several quarters, with BOK, BI, BOT, and BSP easing or signaling cuts and the RBI on hold, lowering both TLD entity borrowing costs and customer financing costs for GSE leases and purchases. This window is at risk if the oil-driven Fed repricing forces regional central banks to reverse course, so the next 90-180 days should be treated as an opportunistic but time-limited refinancing window. BOJ normalization will modestly raise yen funding costs.

Watch 📊 PMI Regional

APAC PMIs mixed; India and Vietnam expand 52-58, Japan-Korea-Thailand stall

Reading
APAC manufacturing PMIs remain bifurcated: India and Vietnam consistently print in expansion at 52-58, Indonesia and the Philippines hover near the 50 line, while Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand oscillate between mild contraction and marginal expansion on weak external demand and soft semiconductor cycles. No single country has breached the 48 contraction or 52 expansion threshold for the first time in 3+ months, so no CRITICAL flag is triggered.
Supply chain impact
The India-Vietnam expansion band supports TLD's China+1 sourcing optionality, with supplier capacity and lead times improving in both markets. Persistent sub-52 readings in Japan, Korea, and Thailand signal softer industrial capex appetite among customers in those markets, which may slow order intake for GSE replacement cycles.
Financing / FX impact
Divergent PMIs imply divergent credit demand: expansion markets (India, Vietnam) will see firmer local lending rates and stronger customer financing appetite, while stall markets (Japan, Korea, Thailand) keep rates accommodative and customer financing conditions easy. Net FX impact is neutral absent a threshold breach.
Recommended action
CFO APAC to track India and Vietnam PMI prints weekly for a sustained move above 55 (upside capex signal) and Japan/Korea/Thailand for a drop below 48 (downside warning); escalate immediately on any first-time breach.
Important 🌍 FDI Regional

China+1 FDI accelerates into Vietnam, India, Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia

Reading
China+1 diversification continues to channel FDI into Vietnam, Indonesia, India, Thailand, and Malaysia, concentrated in electronics, EV, and advanced manufacturing. Vietnam and India are the largest beneficiaries, Thailand attracts Japanese and Chinese auto/EV investment, and Singapore remains the dominant regional financial and holding hub.
Supply chain impact
This is a favorable structural tailwind for TLD's non-China sourcing footprint, expanding the pool of qualified component and sub-assembly suppliers in Vietnam, India, and Thailand and reducing single-country concentration risk. It also creates new industrial customer segments (EV, electronics, advanced manufacturing) in these markets that require GSE-adjacent material handling and logistics equipment.
Financing / FX impact
Rising FDI inflows support local currency stability in Vietnam, India, and Indonesia, reducing FX translation volatility on TLD's EUR-reported results. Stronger FDI also deepens local banking liquidity, improving access to competitive local-currency financing for TLD entities and customers in these markets.
Recommended action
CFO APAC to commission a 30-day supplier-mapping review of Vietnam, India, and Thailand to identify qualified non-China sources and quantify potential landed-cost savings; brief the board on FDI-driven customer segment expansion at the next quarterly review.
