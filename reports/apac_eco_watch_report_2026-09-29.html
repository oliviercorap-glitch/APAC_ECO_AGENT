Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture this week is dominated by monetary easing and trade de-escalation originating in China, with India moving in the opposite direction through a US$20 billion liquidity drain via dollar-rupee swaps. China has cut a key policy rate, added a mortgage subsidy, and signalled a further stimulus package, reinforcing a regional easing bias that weakens the renminbi and pressures other APAC currencies against the dollar. The US-China tariff waiver on a US$30 billion basket of basic goods lowers landed costs for regional supply chains, though the rare earths exclusion preserves a strategic risk for electronics and motor components. For TLD APAC, the net effect is mildly supportive on customer financing conditions and input costs, but negative on EUR translation of regional results if APAC currencies weaken further. India's tighter liquidity is the main offsetting risk, raising local funding costs and rupee sensitivity for TLD's South Asia exposure. Priority actions are to refresh the APAC FX and rate sensitivity model, quantify INR exposure, and confirm tariff-waived component coverage.

Rare earth input risk remains the key unresolved cost variable for regional component sourcing.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions should ease modestly over the next 90-180 days, led by China's rate cut and stimulus bias, which typically pulls APAC funding costs and sovereign spreads lower. Customer financing for GSE leases and purchases across Southeast Asia and Oceania should benefit from cheaper local rates, while India is the exception with tighter rupee liquidity and higher short-term funding costs. TLD's intercompany funding in APAC faces a mild tailwind outside India.
