Executive summary
The APAC ex-China region shows a bifurcated picture with strong manufacturing momentum in India (PMI 55.1, seven-month high) and the Philippines (PMI 54.9, fourth consecutive month of expansion), both breaching the 52 expansion threshold and signalling robust industrial demand. However, Indonesia's FX policy tightening (USD25,000 threshold on FX purchases) and Fiji's FATF grey-listing introduce regulatory and compliance complexity for regional treasury operations. US tariffs continue to reshape Pacific island economies, with Fiji revising growth downward, while EU-China trade tensions add uncertainty to regional supply chain dynamics. For TLD, the net impact is positive on the demand side, with India and Philippines manufacturing strength supporting customer capex appetite, but financing and compliance costs are rising in Indonesia and Oceania. Priority actions include reviewing India and Philippines sales pipelines for upside, ensuring Indonesia FX compliance, and enhancing AML/CFT due diligence for Fijian counterparties.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
India and the Philippines are emerging as the region's strongest manufacturing competitiveness stories, with PMI readings well above 52 supporting industrial capex and potentially attracting further China+1 supply chain relocation. Indonesia's FX controls and Fiji's FATF grey-listing add operational friction and compliance costs in those markets, slightly eroding their relative competitiveness. Overall, the region's manufacturing cost base remains stable to improving, with labour cost advantages in South and Southeast Asia continuing to attract investment.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are mixed, with strong PMI data in India and the Philippines likely keeping central banks cautious on rate cuts, maintaining current borrowing costs for TLD entities and customers. Indonesia's FX controls may increase transaction costs and reduce operational flexibility for cross-border funding. Fiji's FATF grey-listing will tighten financial sector compliance and may increase country risk premiums, while European sovereign yield concerns could raise TLD Group's overall cost of capital and affect intercompany funding rates.
