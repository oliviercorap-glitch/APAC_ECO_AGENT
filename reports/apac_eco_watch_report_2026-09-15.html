China mandates 60-day supplier payment terms with penalties for late payers

Reading
New rules require large Chinese companies to pay suppliers within 60 days, with penalties for deliberate delays and incentives for compliance, aimed at relieving cash-strapped SMEs. Although this is a Mainland China measure, it sets a regional precedent for payment-term regulation that could be copied by APAC regulators and directly affects any TLD entity or supplier transacting with large Chinese counterparties.
Supply chain impact
TLD suppliers with Chinese operations or Chinese-owned parents may see improved working capital and more reliable delivery, reducing upstream disruption risk. Conversely, TLD entities that buy from or sell to large Chinese firms must confirm their own payment practices comply, and any TLD receivables from Chinese counterparties could face new enforcement dynamics.
Financing / FX impact
Faster supplier payment obligations at Chinese counterparties improve the credit profile of TLD's Chinese-linked suppliers, lowering supply-chain finance risk. TLD's own payables and receivables terms with Chinese customers should be reviewed for compliance exposure and cash-conversion-cycle impact.
Recommended action
Regional finance should map all TLD APAC contracts with large Chinese counterparties and confirm payment terms within 60 days; complete the review within two weeks and flag any renegotiation needs.

Watch 💱 FX Regional

BRICS pursues pragmatic de-dollarisation via payment links, no common currency

Reading
Analysts report BRICS is advancing de-dollarisation through bilateral payment linkages and local-currency settlement rather than a single common currency, a more incremental and practical path. The shift is gradual but directionally meaningful for cross-border settlement, invoicing, and reserve composition across member and partner economies including India, Indonesia, and potentially Thailand.
Supply chain impact
Expanding local-currency settlement corridors could lower FX conversion costs and settlement friction for TLD's cross-border equipment and spares flows within Asia, particularly India-Indonesia and intra-ASEAN trade. It may also open alternative payment channels that reduce reliance on USD correspondent banking for regional suppliers.
Financing / FX impact
Gradual de-dollarisation implies a slow reduction in USD invoicing share, which could lower TLD's USD transaction hedging needs over time but introduces new local-currency exposure and basis risk. Treasury should begin tracking which APAC counterparties are settling in local currency to anticipate hedging-mix changes.
Recommended action
Treasury should add a standing agenda item to monitor local-currency settlement adoption among TLD's APAC customers and suppliers, and reassess the USD/EUR hedging mix at the next quarterly review.
