Executive summary
APAC ex-China macro conditions are bifurcated but net constructive for TLD, with India's manufacturing PMI at a seven-month high of 55.1, Vietnam holding expansion at 51.9, and China+1 FDI continuing to flow into Vietnam, Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia, and India. North Asia remains soft, with Japan, Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand PMIs near or below 50 on weak export demand and electronics inventory correction, while the yen sits at 157.2 per USD and the RBA holds at 4.35% with a hike bias. Most regional central banks are easing or on hold, lowering local funding costs, but the overarching risk outlook is deteriorating on renewed USD strength, Fed repricing, tariff escalation, and geopolitical tensions. For TLD, the net effect is a favorable South and Southeast Asian demand and sourcing backdrop offset by North Asian currency and demand weakness and rising regional risk premia. Priority actions are to lock in lower local funding in easing markets, refresh FX hedging for JPY, KRW, and THB exposures, and stress-test FY27 cash flows against a USD-strength and tariff-escalation scenario.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
TLD's regional cost base is under pressure from EUR strength versus JPY, KRW, and THB, which raises the translated cost of euro-denominated intercompany funding and equipment transfers into North Asia. South and Southeast Asian markets remain relatively more stable and competitive, supporting TLD's sourcing and commercial positioning in India, Vietnam, and Indonesia. Over the next 30-90 days, expect continued divergence with North Asian margins compressed and South/Southeast Asian demand resilient.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are broadly improving, with most APAC central banks easing or on hold and funding costs plateauing or declining, a tailwind for TLD's local entity borrowing and customer GSE financing. The two exceptions are the BOJ's gradual normalization and the RBA's cautious hike bias, which could raise local costs in Japan and Australia. Renewed USD strength and Fed repricing remain the key upside risks to regional funding costs over the next 90-180 days.
