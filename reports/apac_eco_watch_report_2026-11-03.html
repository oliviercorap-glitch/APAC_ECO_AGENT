Executive summary
The APAC ex-China region is broadly in expansion, with manufacturing PMIs above 52 in Indonesia, Japan (53.3), Vietnam (52.3), South Korea (51.3), Malaysia (51.0) and Thailand (54.8), while Australia (49.6) and the Philippines (49.1) sit in contraction. India stands out with record FDI of USD 94.5 billion in FY26 and Vietnam attracted USD 21.07 billion in nine-month FDI, 82.6% into manufacturing, confirming the China+1 supply chain shift. Monetary policy is tightening in South Korea (expected 25bp hike to 3.25%) and Australia (25bp hike to 4.85%, fifth of 2026), raising local financing costs, while the Philippines signals a cautious easing path. Hong Kong GDP grew 5.2% YoY and South Korea 1.0% q/q, supporting regional demand. Net impact on TLD APAC is positive on demand and customer base but negative on financing costs in Korea and Australia; priority actions are locking in Korean and Australian funding, reviewing Philippine and Australian customer credit, and confirming BSP registration compliance.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
The region's manufacturing competitiveness is strengthening in Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia and India, supporting TLD's sourcing diversification and customer base outside China over the next 30-90 days. Australia and the Philippines face contractionary pressure that may soften local demand, while tightening in Korea and Australia raises the regional cost base.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are bifurcating, with higher rates in South Korea (toward 3.25%) and Australia (4.85%) raising TLD entity and customer borrowing costs over 90-180 days, while record FDI inflows into India and Vietnam support currency stability and favorable conditions there. The Philippines' cautious easing path keeps local costs elevated in the near term.
