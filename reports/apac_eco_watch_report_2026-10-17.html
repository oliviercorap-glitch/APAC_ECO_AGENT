Executive summary
The APAC ex-China picture is mixed, with Japan manufacturing expanding at a four-year high (PMI 51.6, composite 52.4), India services strong at 55.8 but manufacturing at a near five-year low of 52.8, and Indonesia slipping back into contraction. The most acute risk is the Philippines, where dollar reserves have fallen over USD 13 billion since February on FX intervention, signaling significant peso pressure and eroding external buffers. Vietnam continues to attract high-value FDI (USD 10.76bn, 82.6% of H1 inflows into high-tech manufacturing) while India faces over INR 40,000 crore of foreign equity outflows. Global central banks remain in synchronous tightening, with the Bank of Korea at 3.00% after a 25bp hike and New Zealand inflation at 4.1%, keeping regional financing costs elevated. Priority actions are the Philippine peso exposure escalation within 48 hours, a regional FX hedging review, and updating funding cost assumptions for Korea, New Zealand, and Japan.
🌏 Regional competitiveness outlook (30-90 days)
Vietnam and India remain the primary beneficiaries of China+1 diversification, with Vietnam's high-tech FDI concentration reinforcing its sourcing competitiveness. Thailand's lagging productivity and Indonesia's return to contraction erode their relative manufacturing appeal, while Japan's factory strength supports its industrial base. Net regional cost-base pressure is upward on fuel and logistics, particularly in Oceania.
🏦 Financing / credit outlook (90-180 days)
Regional financing conditions are likely to stay restrictive over the next 90-180 days given synchronous global tightening, a higher-for-longer US rate environment, and above-target inflation in New Zealand. Philippine and Indian country risk premiums are likely to widen on reserve depletion and equity outflows, raising hedging and funding costs. Korean and Japanese rates bear watching for further moves.
